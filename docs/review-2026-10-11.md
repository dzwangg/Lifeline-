## Verdict

The build is healthy and the city is genuinely impressive, but the game has no ending and two of the three sponsor
tracks are invisible or unnamed in the main flow. The overnight push shipped the world, turns, residents and Ask the
city; it did not ship a payoff. A judge who plays it sees: a lovely start screen that doesn't say what to do, a
five-click setup before anything moves, a minute of waiting at 1x, metrics in nominal dollars that look like the
policies tripled rent, and a "Game complete" sentence with a New game button. Gemini narration with ElevenLabs
voice works live on the API (3 s, 780 KB of audio) and nothing on the page calls it. Fix the ending, default to
today's dollars, name the sponsors on screen, raise the per-IP rate limits for the venue, and run the demo at 4x.
Close #26, don't merge #19, and only merge #27 if the team is willing to put its value weights on screen.

### #27 Score, "four ways to lose", discard drafted cards (kevinyuan836, +1368/−74, MERGEABLE, fast-forward)
288/288 tests pass on the branch. Adds `shared/health.js` (pure scorer), two metrics (`realIncome`, `taxBurden`),
four new cards, a score panel and a final-screen headline, and a proper discard flow for drafted cards
(`player-cards.js` rewritten with v3 key, `store.dropPolicy`, trash button with confirm, Undo mapping). Also bumps
a perf guard from 3 s to 8 s and exempts the set-aside card from a resident invariant.

**The score.** Purely relative to the do-nothing run: per indicator, `move = clamp((value − base) × dir / swing)`,
score `50 + 50·move`; Economy half (6 indicators, equal weight) and Residents half (11 indicators, weights 0.5 to
2) averaged. Bands 72/58/43/28 are unsourced. Swings are tuned to what the model does, by the doc's own admission.
"Four ways to lose" are not loss conditions; they are four cards that score under 50 at default: inclusionary
set-aside 39, longer development review 46, impact fee 47, free downtown parking 49. The game still ends after
three decades whatever the score.

**Whose values.** Residents half: lower rent burden (weight 2), fewer priced out (1.5), more tree cover (1.5),
**lower property tax burden (1.5)**, lower price-to-income, shorter commute, more bus trips, fewer car commuters,
less CO₂. Economy half: more homes, jobs, storefronts, real income, and **more Town net revenue**. So a tax cut
scores 49 and an 8¢ tax rise scores 54, "better than doing nothing"; density, canopy and mode shift are good; the
losing cards are inclusionary zoning, more hearings, impact fees and free parking. That is a market-urbanist plus
environmentalist objective function with a taxpayer term bolted on. Any of a fiscal conservative, a
neighbourhood-preservation homeowner, a tenants' advocate who wants inclusionary zoning, or a car commuter can
read the number as taking a side. The doc says "the weights are where the value judgement is deliberately loud".

**Is that visible to the player?** No. Weights appear only inside hover tooltips for the few indicators whose
`why` text mentions them; `target`/`floor` are computed and never rendered; the player-facing note says
"docs/health-score.md has the method", a repo path. The player cannot change the weights.

**Bugs found on the branch.** `taxBurden`'s "today" reference (3.4%, Town rate over median income) is a different
measure from what `combined.js:133` computes (Town + county rate over owner income, 4.47% at year 0). `realIncome`
is deflated twice when "today's dollars" is on. The free-parking card's con claims the lost fees "come off the
property tax"; the lever only changes `netFiscal`. The lowest-scoring card simulates a mandate the card's own con
says NC law forbids. No 30-year score test; the final headline can show a stale run if a forecast is in flight.

**Recommendation.** Merge only with: weights shown on screen (a column in "Every indicator"), the "docs/" line
removed from player copy, the taxBurden and double-deflation fixes, and either a player-adjustable weight set or
the two halves shown without a single 0–100 number. As a nonpartisan pitch, a hidden single objective function
that penalises inclusionary zoning and rewards tax rises is the one thing a judge can use against you. The
discard-drafted-cards part is good and independent; it could be split out and merged alone (S).
